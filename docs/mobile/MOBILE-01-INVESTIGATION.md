# MOBILE-01 — the two intermittent failures of the acceptance run, investigated

Both were open items in `docs/proof/playable/ACCEPTANCE.md` § 5–6 (the VTC drop-off stall; the `check:online` rapide timeout). Both were investigated **without blocking the preview**, by reading the code and by simulation on the real modules in Node (no browser, no device). Each investigation was done by a delegated agent and its citations were spot-checked here; where something is the agent's estimate or an inference, it is marked. **Neither is shown on a phone. Neither is a game defect as far as this goes.** The two reports leave one thing a device can settle (§ 3).

## 1. `check:online`: the car-rapide wait that timed out in CI (run 42342072205, `242cc74e`)

**Verdict: a race in the check, not a bug in the game.**

- `scripts/check-multiplayer.mjs` (before the fix, lines 149–154) reads `nextAt(line, stop 0)`, warps the line's clock to 6 s before that arrival if it is above 8 s, then waits 60 s for B to be `riding`.
- `nextAt` (`src/transport/module.ts:363-372`, `nextArrival`) reads each car's state **as of the page's last frame** and answers **0** for a car standing at the stop with more than `MIN_STOP` of its dwell left. B can only be taken by `TripState.step` (`src/transport/trip.ts:49-51`: `dwell === stop && dwellLeft > MIN_STOP`), on the **next** frame. On a slow page (the CI's second page took 32 s to come online) that frame can come after the car has left. B stays `waiting`; the next car is a lap away (a single car at low quality, period 120 s); the check had read 0, so it did not warp; 60 s cannot reach it.
- That is the CI signature: `waiting`, the car already heading for stop 3 (67.75 s after its arrival, a time a warp cannot explain), `next` about 52 s.
- **Reproduced in Node** on the real `TransportModule` and the real Pikine district (`tests/perfHost.ts`) with the check's exact logic: with 33 start phases per cadence, 0 misses at 0.033, 0.25, 0.5 and 1 s a frame; exactly one miss at each of 2, 4 and 8 s a frame, with `wait0 = 0.0`. A miss happens iff the frame gap exceeds the car's dwell time left minus 1.6 s. Estimated odds (the agent's): about 7.5 % of runs have a car standing when B starts to wait, times a frame-gap factor: 1–4 % a run, which fits one red in about fifteen.
- **Not proved by observation:** the failing run did not print `wait0` or the car's dwell, so "consistent with the CI's state and reproduced on the same module" is the strength of the claim. The next red run, if there is one, would settle it with one log line (`wait0` and `vehicles[0].dwellLeft` right after the read).
- **For a real player** it is cosmetic: `wantBoard` (`module.ts:488-493`) says « Le 23 est là : on monte ! », the car leaves, the card then says « Prochain car rapide dans ≈ N s ». Nothing sticks, nothing is charged wrongly. An optional tidy is to make the promise true (not done: it is a game change).
- **Fix, on PR #90 (the CI branch), commit `f0266bfa`:** read, warp, give the arrival 20 s to take B, and read and warp again if B is still waiting (at most four times); the last wait for `riding` is unchanged. It proves the same thing: a game that never takes a waiting passenger fails all four tries and that wait. In the simulation it boards in at most two tries at every cadence. The script passed locally on the final tree: 46 checks, 0 failures. One CI run cannot prove a 1–4 % fix; the evidence is the simulation and the next runs.
- The same read-then-warp pattern is in `scripts/check-transport.mjs:37,73` and `scripts/check-arena-arrival.mjs:140`. They are not run in CI and were not changed.

## 2. The VTC drop-off stall (`final-tree/vtc.log`, 14/19)

**Verdict: a limitation of the scripted driver, reproduced headlessly; that the driver was slow at that moment is inferred, not observed.**

- **What stopped the car:** it ended at (−54.8, −51.9), nose east, against the **west wall of the Grande Mosquée's courtyard** (`src/venues/mosque.ts:160`, `wallSeg(-22.75, …)`, collider x −52.9…−52.6, z −52.85…−7.15, 2.1 m high), about 1 m south of its north-west corner. It got there by overshooting the left turn at the Plateau junction (−60, −60).
- `pilot()` (`scripts/lib/realTouch.mjs:109`) has **no reverse**, and the last leg gives it `stall: 60000` (`scripts/check-vtc-ride.mjs`), so it sat there until its waypoints timed out. **A human escapes:** holding the brake at a standstill reverses after 0.7 s (`src/transport/controls.ts`, `REVERSE_DELAY`); in the simulation the car backs out of every pinned pose in 2–3 s.
- **Reproduced:** the agent ported `pilot()` and ran it against the real `driveSteps`, `DriveControls` and the Plateau's colliders. With the driver's loop at 0.6 s or faster (game time), the car arrives every time at any frame rate; at a 1.0 s loop 8–22 % of runs fail, at 1.5 s about half; every failure ends near x ≈ −54.7, z −42…−53, yaw ≈ 2, after 2–4 contacts of 1–5 m/s: the log's pose. A single 2 s stall of the driver before the junction gives (−54.8, −52.0). Low frame rate alone does not do it (the game then runs slower than the clock); a slow Node/CDP side while the page runs normally does, which is what CPU starvation by concurrent builds looks like (the acceptance run overlapped with such work).
- **Day and events:** the run is on city day 1930 (03:47–04:00 UTC; the day rolled to 1931 at 04:00, about four minutes after the car was pinned); the hour is pinned at 8. No Plateau road event on days 1926–1931 is near the route (`src/city/roadEvents.ts`: events are barred from every edge of the route; `closedZones` sees only built events, a latent gap that does not apply here). Builder, parked-car and street-dress solids do not reach the pose; decorative traffic is visual only.
- **Ranked causes (the agent's estimates, not measurements):** a pilot-loop stall at the junction about 70 %; a touch/pad desync (the page drops a finger the script thinks is down) about 15 %; the line-5 car rapide sharing the lane about 10 % (in a scan of all 117 timetable phases it caused small hits in about 4 % of phases and never a failure); a rollover, a road event or a ghost collider under 5 %.
- **Proposed, not applied** (they change a driver shared by many checks, and a retry could hide a real wedge; the owner has not asked): an opt-in unstick in `pilot()` (after 5 s without moving, hold the real brake with opposite lock for about 3.5 s, then resume, logging each use); in the VTC journey `stall: 8000` and up to three retries of the last leg with a fresh route; cruise 4–5 on that leg (60/60 arrivals at a 1.0 s loop against 45/60 at cruise 7).
- **The stall dump is not enough to settle a recurrence:** it keeps only the last 60 s of the trace (all of it the pinned pose), the impacts have no timestamp, and nothing records the pilot's own loop time, the commands it sent, the pad's actual steer/gas/brake, or the car rapide's pose. Keeping the full 4 Hz trace and adding those fields is the cheap next step.
- **Model limits:** no pedestrians, no other modules' colliders, no rendering; the corridor mouth is approximated; the driver's timing is a parameter, not a measurement.

### 2 bis. What was done about it (release sprint, branch `wp/drive`)

The investigation above stands; this is what changed since. Nothing here was seen on a device.

- **Game side: was it a game weakness?** Partly, and that part is fixed. A human could always back out, but the recovery was slow and the contact itself was wrong: (1) a wall met **exactly square on** the grid's axes (the common case in a district: every wall runs along x or z) was treated as a « slide on the spot »: no rebound, the car left touching the wall, the knock reported as a scrape; (2) any slide lost 40 % of the speed on every frame, so a glancing touch of that wall's corner was a dead stop too; (3) the reverse needed a 0.7 s hold and then built up at 1.6 m/s². Now a wall met square on stops the car and rolls it back a hand's width, the brake backs it out after 0.15 s at 3.2 m/s² (2.5 m clear in 1.8 s; it was 2.4 s), and a glancing contact turns the nose along the wall and keeps about 90 % of the speed (`src/transport/drive.ts`, `controls.ts`; `tests/collisionFeel.test.ts`). The mosque's wall and the drop-off kerb were **not** moved (the world is not this package's): the wedge needed an overshoot of the junction, which a late scripted driver makes and a human rarely does.
- **Script side** (`scripts/lib/realTouch.mjs` `pilot()`): an **opt-in** `unstick` (off by default: every other journey drives as before): after 5 game seconds without moving, the gas is lifted, the wheel turned the other way and the **real brake pedal** held until the car has backed about 4 m (3.5 game seconds at most), then the route goes on; each use is printed (`pilot: UNSTICK …`) and counted (`pilot.unsticks`), so a wedge is survived and reported, never hidden. The pedal is used as an analog pedal (the toe where the car settles at the wanted speed by itself), which also removed the gas-brake lurching that the quicker cars would have had. An opt-in `trace` records the driver's own loop: both clocks, the pose, the thumbs, the loop time.
- **The journey** (`scripts/check-vtc-ride.mjs`): the last leg is driven at 5 m/s (7), with `stall: 8000` (60 000), the unstick, and up to **three tries** with a fresh route from where the car stands. Every check of the ride is unchanged (the passenger, the fare paid once, the reloads, one person in the scene). The **whole leg's 4 Hz trace**, the driver's log and the impacts go to `last-leg-trace.json`; the impacts are stamped by the vehicle itself with the game time and the pose (`feel().impacts[i].t/x/z/yaw`); a `STALL` dump prints the last minute at 4 Hz and the driver's last 40 loops.
- **Observed here** (headless, software renderer): `scripts/probe-vtc-dropoff.mjs` (the same leg without the passenger) arrived at the kerb twice out of twice (0.3 m and 0.7 m from the drop-off), no contact, no unstick, a median driver loop of 94–95 ms. Two runs do not measure a failure that was intermittent; the result of the full journey is in the release report.

## 3. What a device can settle

The only part of the VTC question that is about **players** is whether a person who overshoots that junction and ends against the mosque's wall can get out. The simulation says the brake reverses the car after 0.7 s. MOBILE-01.md has an optional step for it (step 8b): take a VTC ride to the Grande Mosquée; if the car ever stops against a wall short of the drop-off, say where and whether the brake got it out.

## 4. What this changes in the acceptance report

Nothing is rewritten: `docs/proof/playable/ACCEPTANCE.md` stands as accepted. Read § 5.1 and § 6 of it with this page: the VTC stall is a driver limitation reproduced in Node, still unobserved on a device; the `check:online` timeout is a race in the check with a fix on PR #90, not yet seen to hold in CI.
